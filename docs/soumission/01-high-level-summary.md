## Texte à coller

Space Heroes is a browser game about the work that happens before a science mission ever flies. The player takes one seat on a small team, states the science question, and designs a spacecraft against six limits that move together: budget, mass, power, data, communications, and time. The launch that follows is not a painted sky. It plays a trajectory propagated in NASA's General Mission Analysis Tool. The debrief then places the result next to a real NASA mission.

The project answers the Design and Develop Science Missions challenge by making the design trade playable. A Concept Maturity Level gate has to be green before the team may leave a phase. Parts come from real classes: propulsion, structure, power, communications, and instrument. Three tests stand between the workshop and the pad. Eight missions are playable, from a 4 kg low-Earth CubeSat to the Moon, Mars, a Sun-Earth L1 halo, a polar orbit, a high orbit, and geostationary orbit. One player can fly alone. Four players on the same network can share the gauges.

What matters is that a student, or a judge, can finish a mission on a laptop without installing a flight-dynamics tool, and can still see where each figure came from. The orbits were computed in GMAT R2026a and stored for the game to display. The browser does not invent a new lunar capture, a new Mars window, or a new station contact. Where the project uses an assumption, as with the four station-keeping maneuvers a year at L1, the screen says so.
