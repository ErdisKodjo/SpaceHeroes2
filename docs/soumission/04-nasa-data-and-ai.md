## Texte à coller

This project uses NASA data for the numbers a player sees. The trajectories are outputs of the General Mission Analysis Tool, release R2026a, propagated for this project on 15 November 2026. The game stores those reports and displays them. The browser does not launch GMAT. That is the data use that makes the project eligible for Global Awards consideration.

Low Earth orbit. An 84-day propagation grid gives the drag loss on four CubeSat faces. At 1.33 kilograms the 3U face, 0.03405 square meters, reaches 200 kilometers altitude on day 114.8305515255815, shown in the interface as day 114.8. The 200 kilometer stop is a limit chosen for the lesson. The Debris Assessment Software is named beside it because that software is used to judge end of life. DAS did not produce this grid.

The Moon. Departure is 1 January 2026 00:00:00. Parking orbit semi-major axis 6,678 kilometers, inclination 28.5 degrees, right ascension of the ascending node 355 degrees. Injection in the velocity frame is plus 3.12 kilometers per second. Arrival is at 7.817 days, with a perilune altitude of 49.98 kilometers, shown as 50 kilometers, and a lunar radius distance of 1,788 kilometers. The capture burn is 0.87 kilometers per second. At 16.517 days the propagated altitude is 37.174 kilometers, shown as 37 kilometers, and the spacecraft is still bound. These capture figures come from GMAT.

Mars. The orbit period, from one periapsis to the next, is 6,800 seconds, shown as 113.3 minutes. The departure window falls in early December 2026. Opposition is 20 February 2027, at 0.68 astronomical units. At the far point of that cycle, 10 May 2028, the light time is 20 minutes one way and 40 minutes for the reply.

Polar orbit. Period 5,669.54 seconds, shown as 94.5 minutes, at 500 kilometers and 90 degrees inclination. For that plan, Svalbard sees about 15 passes a day.

High orbit and geostationary orbit. Both last 1,436.0 minutes at 35,786 kilometers. For the propagated departure, Canberra sees the geostationary satellite for 1,440 minutes without a gap, and sees 790 minutes of the high orbit at 28 degrees inclination. The other stations in the game see none of that plan. This is not a claim about every geostationary longitude.

Sun-Earth L1. The distance of 1.5 million kilometers and the halo of 173 days come from the GMAT run. Four corrections a year are an operational hypothesis. They do not come from the sample targeter, and the game says so in the legend, the debrief, the launch screen, and the hub.

The optional orrery does not replace those trajectories. Earth and Mars use the Keplerian elements published by JPL for 1800–2050, fitted by Standish and Williams to the mean ecliptic and equinox of J2000. The lunar angle uses Fred Espenak's new moon of 8 January 2008.4849 Dynamical Time and the sidereal month of 27.32166 days. It is an approximate clock for a drawing, not a flight ephemeris.

Public tools are named and not credited with the figures. The Trajectory Browser is the public catalogue of trajectories to planets and small bodies. PyCubed is described as one public board for power, computing, communication, and attitude control. SPENVIS is named as the model of the radiation environment on the shield. None of the four produced a number in the tables.

Concept Maturity Levels follow the JPL practice described by Wessen, Borden, Ziemer, and Kwok in 2013. The game uses a 20-line checklist and an 80 percent green gate. It is a teaching cut of that practice, not the full matrix.

Artificial intelligence. The shipped game does not call a generative model. ASTRA speaks lines written for each step, in French and in English, through the browser speech synthesis. An AI coding assistant in the editor was used to implement features, tests, and these submission texts. Orbital numbers were not asked of that assistant. They were copied from the GMAT reports and from the JPL and GSFC pages listed in the references.
